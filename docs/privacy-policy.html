Privacy Policy for UDPCore

Last updated: 25 August 2026

UDPCore ("the app") is an Android application that sends control commands to microcontrollers and other hardware you own, either over UDP on your local Wi-Fi network or over a Bluetooth Classic connection. This policy explains what the app does and does not do with your information.

Summary

UDPCore does not collect, store, transmit, or share any personal data. There are no user accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting, and no third-party tracking of any kind. The developer has no server and receives no information from the app.

Information the app stores

The app saves your configuration on your device only, using Android's standard app-private storage. This includes:

  • The target IP address and port you enter
  • The command strings assigned to each control
  • The send interval and related transmission preferences
  • The name and Bluetooth address of a device you select, so it can reconnect
  • Your slider configuration and control-surface preference

This data never leaves your device. It is not backed up to any service controlled by the developer, and it is deleted when you uninstall the app or clear its storage.

Information the app transmits

The only data the app sends is the control commands you trigger, and those go directly to the destination you configure yourself — an IP address on your local network, or a Bluetooth device you have paired. Nothing is routed through the developer or through any third party.

Permissions and why they are needed

  • Internet / Network state — to open a UDP socket and send command packets to the device address you specify on your local network.
  • Bluetooth (connect, scan) — to list paired devices, discover nearby devices when you press Scan, and open a serial connection to the device you choose.
  • Approximate/precise location (Android 11 and older only) — Android versions up to and including Android 11 require location permission before an app is allowed to scan for nearby Bluetooth devices. UDPCore uses it for that reason alone. The app does not read, record, store, or transmit your location. On Android 12 and newer this permission is not requested at all.

Children

UDPCore is a hardware control utility and is not directed at children. It collects no data from any user, regardless of age.

Third-party services

The app uses open-source Flutter libraries for local storage, Bluetooth serial communication, and Android permission handling. None of them collect or transmit user data on the developer's behalf.
